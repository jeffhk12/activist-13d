Phase 1 / What we built

A reproducible test of investing after the disclosure

The strategy asks what an outside investor could earn after the filing became public. It does not assume access to the fund’s original purchase price, full portfolio, hedges or actual exit decisions.

Find eligible opportunitiesScreen source filings, separate initials from amendments, identify first observed investor–company pairs, and match each filing to the historical stock.
Measure stock performanceCompare event returns with historically assigned Russell size-segment ETF proxies; retain SPY context and industry/year/size comparisons.
Simulate a funded accountApply position limits, liquidity constraints, costs, overlapping-position rules, cash interest and corporate-action accounting.

Investor universe

We examined a broader HFR-linked manager universe and a focused activist-candidate subset, supplemented by selected manager groups. The focused subset overlaps the broader group; their counts cannot be added.

Manager links remain candidates. Related funds, controllers and aliases need historical verification. A filer not matched to HFR is not automatically passive or non-activist.

Trading conventions

The long-horizon test uses entry at the opening price on the second exchange session after the acceptance date under the frozen calendar rule, with 5% issuer targets and a maximum of 20 issuers.

$100,000 is the primary account; $100 million scenarios examine capacity. Observed opening prices are modeled execution assumptions. Fixed holding periods do not imitate the manager’s actual exits.

First observed means first in our restricted archive, not proof of a first-ever campaign. Protected human-review issuers remain excluded; Hill International is a disclosed retrospective exclusion. E1, E2, E4 and E6.

Interpretation: A minority of large winners can lift the average while most opportunities disappoint. That motivates a selection test, but it does not show that the winners—or their managers—were identifiable in advance.

Samples overlap and differ across horizons. On the same 50 focused events calculable at all four horizons, mean cumulative excess is +7.13, +8.97, +18.24 and +20.83 points respectively. This is a coverage diagnostic: selecting only events with all future outcomes available would not be an investable rule.

Why annualized and cumulative averages can tell different stories

At two years, averaging each event’s annualized return gives 4.95% for the stocks and 8.38% for the proxies, a −3.43-point difference. Annualizing each observation and then averaging is different from averaging cumulative returns first. Unequal outcomes and the nonlinear calculation explain the sign change. Neither statistic is a funded-account CAGR.

Proposed Phase 1 extension / Not yet run

What if we follow only the top-quartile managers?

The hypothesis: managers with stronger historically observable post-disclosure investment records may generate better subsequent opportunities. This is a useful bridge from simple following to Phase 2, without requiring a complex predictive model.

What we would rate

The return an outsider could earn after each manager’s eligible public disclosures, relative to the appropriate benchmark. This is a disclosure-following track record, not the fund’s reported performance or proof of manager skill.

What we have not established

No validated top-quartile list, persistence result or selected-manager portfolio has been produced. Ranking managers on the whole sample and then reporting that sample’s winners would describe hindsight.

Check whether ranking is feasible

Build a manager-by-year table of verified identities, completed prior observations, coverage and subsequent disclosures. The 56 focused two-year events must support both ranking histories and later tests; many managers may have too little evidence.

Freeze a ranking rule

Propose annual ranking by prior benchmark-relative returns. Choose the lookback, minimum completed history, treatment of missing outcomes, ties and new managers before measuring the strategy. Reduce the influence of noisy small-sample estimates by pulling them toward the group average.

Select using information available then

At each annual cutoff, select the top 25% of eligible managers using only returns whose holding windows have already ended. Freeze membership for new signals during the following year.

Follow subsequent disclosures consistently

Retain Phase 1 entry, holding, benchmark, 5% position, liquidity and cost conventions. Specify how manager re-ranking affects existing positions; a fixed-holding baseline should not silently acquire a new exit rule.

Test whether the ranking persists

Compare subsequent top-quartile opportunities with all eligible managers and the remaining managers over common dates. Report both stock-level results and funded portfolios, including cash, turnover, drawdowns, concentration and uncertainty.

A timing example: At January 1, 2023, a filing entered in mid-2022 has no completed two-year return. Its eventual 2024 outcome cannot help rank that manager in 2023. Longer horizons reduce the usable ranking history, even when the full dataset looks large.

The quartile cutoff, ranking score and annual schedule are proposals, not frozen choices. A broader manager universe may improve sample size but changes the research question. We should decide eligibility from evidence and feasibility, rather than expand the sample until a profitable result appears.

This extension reflects the September 30 discussion. Existing outcomes have already been examined, so a historical rolling test would be exploratory development. Final validation requires an exposure audit and, if necessary, a prospective protocol.

Future phases / Earn the added complexity

First improve the data, then test the information

Phase 2: Does structured information improve selection?

The model would use information known at disclosure to rank opportunities. Inputs include manager identity and prior activity, attributable ownership, company size and industry, liquidity, volatility and prior returns. Historical manager performance could become one feature, with the top-quartile rule serving as a transparent comparison.

Proposed modeling sequence: start with regularized linear regression, then boosted trees. The proposed target is 504-session cumulative return relative to the historical Russell ETF proxy. The target, missing-data policy and evaluation rules still need to be frozen. A neural network is conditional on sample size and course requirements.

Passing extraction checks is not the same as understanding every filing correctly. The audit checked source references, schemas, citations and internal consistency. Ownership coverage varies by year. Without investigating this, a model could learn document-format differences instead of investment quality.

Phase 3

Disclosed investment intentions

Extract stated objectives such as board representation, capital return or a sale, with exact evidence and uncertainty.

Hold Phase 2 preparation fixed and test whether adding intent improves predictions and net investment results on comparable samples. Any benefit from identity preparation must be distinguished from the benefit of intent.

Evaluation: train on earlier observations whose returns are already known; separate overlapping outcome windows; compare the same trading rules and tuning budgets. Report prediction quality and economic results. Previously inspected history cannot be relabeled an untouched final test. Pretrained language models may know later events, which adds a separate hindsight risk.

Preparation status and proposal: E3 and E4. Independent review, frozen extraction rules and separate evaluation are prerequisites for accuracy claims. Paid model APIs remain off under the project rules.

How to read these numbers
  • Event return: an equally weighted opportunity’s stock return relative to its ETF proxy over the stated horizon, gross of strategy costs.
  • Funded portfolio: the whole account, including capital constraints, modeled costs, cash, overlapping positions and corporate actions.
  • Benchmark: Russell size-segment ETF proxies based on historical monthly membership, not exact index returns; SPY is market context.
  • Long horizons: 252 / 378 / 504 / 756 exchange sessions approximate 12 / 18 / 24 / 36 months. Only calendar-mature opportunities enter the corresponding schedules.
  • Missing data: unresolved returns or settlements remain unresolved. They are not zero returns, proof of poor investments or grounds for convenient exclusion.
  • Research inference: positive historical mean excess is not automatically risk-adjusted alpha, evidence of causation or a repeatable edge.
