The investment thesis

Use machine learning and natural language processing to uncover long-term alpha signals in public companies backed by high-quality event-driven or activist hedge funds, then take active risk in several selected, potentially concentrated positions. The research focuses on stock selection, conviction, downside and position sizing. Defining manager quality, validating incremental selection value, and evaluating shared exposures across positions are research questions. Concentrated sizing has not been tested.

Fordham identifies Michael Sternberg as a Managing Director and head of Aladdin Financial Engineering at BlackRock. His listed background spans quantitative analytics and risk analytics. That suggests emphasizing target definition, scenario modeling and credible validation in this conversation; it does not establish a particular view on activism or his involvement with the MASS role. A 45-second opening

My goal is to use ML and NLP to find long-term investment signals in companies backed by high-quality event-driven or activist hedge funds, then take active risk in several selected positions. I've built a historical 13D-following baseline, prepared about 15,800 filing candidates, and added dated HFR scorecards. Simple following has not established an edge: the two-year average is positive, but the median is negative. I now want to test whether manager quality, company economics and the investor's stated plan help select better opportunities. I'd value your view on how to define quality, test incremental alpha and translate conviction and downside into position sizes.

How it connects to private-markets modeling

The investable security here is a public equity. The connection to this class is a value-creation framework: a change in governance, operations, asset sales or capital allocation could alter cash flows, financing and exit value. Ask how to turn a disclosed plan into scenarios with execution probability, timing and downside. A filing's objective alone does not supply those probabilities or establish that its upside is absent from the price.

Three proposed tests, in a practical order

  1. Investor information: compare following everyone eligible with a fixed rule using historically available manager information. Keep HFR fund-return rankings separate from a manager's prior post-disclosure target-stock outcomes. Fund returns can reflect other positions, hedges and fee structures; they are not the outside investor's replication return. The new scorecards are descriptive preparation, with unverified availability dates and incomplete dead-fund coverage.
  2. Intent plus company economics: compare a structured model with the same model plus evidence-backed disclosed objectives. Candidate inputs include specificity of the plan, voting influence, prior engagement, valuation, leverage and liquidity. These are proposals; lagged market features and validated intent extraction have not been completed.
  3. Amendment-driven position adjustments: test adding, trimming or exiting after public stake/intent updates. Build and validate filing chains and explicit exit labels; these are unfinished. Keep actual transaction dates separate from disclosure availability and freeze trading rules before comparison with fixed holding periods. The handout explains what the reporting threshold can reveal. SEC guidance, especially 103.08, 104.05 and 110.05/08; 2023 final rule on amendment timing (checked October 4, 2026).

The central distinction to discuss

Announcement-window evidence does not establish returns available after our delayed purchase. Earlier research finds substantial price moves around activism announcements, including movement before the filing. Its results motivate the question but do not validate our newer sample or strategy. Brav, Jiang, Partnoy and Thomas, The Returns to Hedge Fund Activism (2008), pp. 1-2.

Our post-disclosure rule enters at the observed opening price on the second exchange session after acceptance under the frozen calendar convention. That is a modeled execution assumption. Ask whether any plausible advantage comes from underappreciated value creation, execution quality, or simply exposure to known risks. The project has not distinguished those explanations.

HFR manager identification and the path to 56 events

Phase 1 uses candidate SEC-to-HFR name links from the latest prior quarterly snapshot under unverified delivery-date proxies. Exact normalized firm/fund names and legal-suffix variants are matched; HFR Firm_ID groups offerings. Strategy labels describe offerings, not the intention in every filing or verified manager quality.

If he challenges the positive average

That is exactly why I haven't claimed alpha. Across 56 calculable two-year focused events, mean gross excess is +8.66 percentage points, but median excess is -14.91, only 41.1% outperform, and the interval is wide. It motivates testing selection; it does not show that I could identify the winners in advance.

Those 56 events are part of a 67-event scheduled focused subset in the 2018-2025 research window. The full schedule has 2,107 events, of which 694 have calculable benchmark comparisons. Counts from different horizons overlap. None is a count of independent managers. Event results are gross cumulative stock-minus-Russell-ETF-proxy returns, not account CAGR or risk-adjusted alpha.

If he asks what is unfinished

Historical manager identities and availability dates still need validation. Filing checks verify provenance and internal consistency, not complete semantic extraction. Ownership overlap needs careful attribution. Predictive models have not been fitted; intent extraction has no validated accuracy result. The historical data have already been examined, so honest development tests and a separately frozen final or prospective evaluation are required.

What you want to leave with

One prioritized economic mechanism; one feasible signal or target; and the evidence he would require before treating a favorable result as investable. If he is interested, offer a short follow-up after implementing that specific test. Keep the career conversation as a separate, direct ask after the research discussion.

Research phases and data provenance

Phase 1: simple post-disclosure following has been evaluated historically; gross event-return evidence has not established an edge. Phase 2: structured filing data, prior-disclosure histories and HFR scorecards are prepared; historical identity joins and company features need work, and no ML model is fitted. Phase 3: extract and validate Item 4 intentions, then test their added value on comparable samples; this remains planned. The ownership/identity pilot supports data preparation and is separate from incremental intent testing.

SEC EDGAR supplies filings and public acceptance timestamps. CRSP through WRDS supplies the daily stock/ETF market data behind the reported return results. FTSE Russell through WRDS supplies historical constituent snapshots for benchmark assignments. HFR supplies the 35 user-provided quarterly fund/manager snapshots. Compustat through WRDS is a planned fundamentals source with access tested, not a finished ML panel. WRDS means Wharton Research Data Services, the access platform.
