Four investment decisions and the model target

A feature is an input known at the decision time. A target is the answer attached to a historical training example. A prediction is the model's estimate for a new example. A policy converts estimates into actions under costs and risk limits.

Investment decisionFirst experimentPossible later learning task
Whether to investPredict benchmark-relative return, then apply a predeclared selection ruleClassification of a defined investment outcome or utility-based selection
How long to holdUse a specified holding period; two years is the proposed primary targetPredict returns at several horizons on a consistent entry sample and compare horizon policies
When to enterUse the existing delayed-entry convention consistentlyPredict results for specified entry delays using only information available at each decision
How much to allocateRetain 5% position targets and existing liquidity/risk constraintsUse validated return/risk estimates in an allocation rule; directly learned sizing requires its own objective and constraints

These decisions jointly define a strategy, but do not automatically supply one supervised-learning label. Historical returns do not uniquely establish the best size or timing: a larger winning position also consumes capital and increases risk. A joint action model requires defined utility, constraints and comparable action outcomes. The first experiment isolates selection so we can understand and measure it.

Model comparison and what each method teaches

MethodIntuitionRole
Training-mean baselinePredict the average known training outcome for every new exampleCheck whether learning features helps at all
Regularized regressionCombine features with learned weights while penalizing excessive weightsTransparent supervised comparison
Gradient-boosted treesCombine small decision trees that successively improve predictionsComparison for nonlinear relationships and interactions
Small feedforward neural networkLearn weighted combinations of inputs through a hidden layer and a prediction outputCentral neural method, with capacity constrained by the sample

Start with a continuous return target rather than inventing an invest/not-invest label from hindsight. The proposed first output is 504-session cumulative stock return minus its assigned Russell ETF-proxy return. Consider one small hidden layer; freeze architecture, regularization, loss, seeds and tuning budget after counting features and usable inner folds. A neural network's method is learned from training examples; being newer is not evidence of better performance.

Teach loss as the error the model tries to reduce; overfitting as learning historical quirks that fail on new examples; and regularization as limiting that flexibility. Fit scaling, imputation and any feature selection using training observations only. Use chronological inner validation for tuning and stopping, not a random early-stopping split. Specify dependencies between issuers/managers and overlapping return windows explicitly.

Proposed reported metrics are mean absolute prediction error, root mean squared error and rank correlation, alongside the mean-prediction baseline and results by fold. Ranking asks whether higher predictions identify better subsequent outcomes. Economic evaluation translates a frozen selection rule into portfolio results with existing costs, cash and capacity constraints. Aratana accounting remains a limitation on affected funded accounts.

Deterministic and LLM feature experiments

  1. A: deterministic features. Filing facts and strictly prior history, plus verified pre-entry company features as they become ready. Keep reporter ownership separate; CIKs are identifiers, not numerical magnitudes. Preserve missingness and distinguish source gaps from zero values.
  2. B: A plus ownership/identity interpretation. Add validated, evidence-backed LLM information about attribution and overlapping holdings.
  3. C: B plus stated intentions. Add validated Item 4 categories with actor and company attribution. Compare identical model families, folds and tuning budgets on common observations; separately report changes in usable coverage.

Use a bounded, human-reviewed extraction pilot before expanding. Record model/prompt/settings and each raw interpretation. Repeated extraction can measure disagreement and how it changes model results; disagreement is measurement uncertainty, and agreement does not prove correctness. Preserve empirical label distributions and abstentions without calling them calibrated probabilities. A proposed repeat scheme requires a new versioned protocol and has not been run.

Learning sequence and remaining decisions

At each implementation step, explain one new concept, show a small 13D example, connect it to the code, and interpret the resulting diagnostic. Begin with features/targets and chronological training; then regression and loss; then hidden layers, activations, gradient descent and backpropagation; then validation, regularization, comparisons and LLM measurement uncertainty. Learn equations and code after the intuition is clear.
