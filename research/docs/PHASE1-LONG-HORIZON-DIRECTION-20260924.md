# Phase 1 — expand the historical period and holding horizons

24 September 2026. User requested longer historical coverage and longer holding periods after reviewing short-horizon results. Direction accepted for design/feasibility; new horizon rankings and production samples are not yet frozen. No longer-horizon returns have been calculated.

## Investment question

Does following an identified investor's initial 13D create value over the time needed for a campaign to develop? Six- and twelve-month tests describe those horizons only; they do not settle a two- or three-year holding thesis. Longer holdings also increase market exposure and company risk, and need not produce higher excess returns.

Long-term activism research has examined five years after intervention: [Bebchuk, Brav and Jiang, The Long-Term Effects of Hedge Fund Activism, NBER Working Paper 21227](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21227). This supports studying longer horizons, not choosing an optimal holding period or assuming a tradable post-disclosure edge. Anticipated benefits may already be reflected in the disclosure price.

## Recommended new exploratory branch

- Broaden the historical research window toward March 2018–December 2025, consistent with the existing common HFR period. The filing archive reaches further back, but earlier manager/identity/benchmark coverage needs separate assessment before extending pre-2018.
- Propose 24 months as the primary long-horizon test, with 12, 18 and 36 months supporting. Preserve the original 3/6/12-month results; do not retrospectively rename the winning horizon primary. Five years can be a later extension if enough earlier events and corporate-action coverage exist.
- Use horizon-specific calendar maturity and also a common 36-month-mature sample across horizons. Compare the same events as well as broader horizon-specific samples. Rebuild issuer nonoverlap schedules for longer positions; do not reset holdings on amendments or repeat signals.
- Retain the fixed investor list, conservative history attribution, human protections, Hill exclusion, Russell/SPY methodology, costs and 5% targets initially. Test concentration separately so the effect of holding period is interpretable.
- Longer holding can increase concurrent exposure but does not create additional signals. It also reduces the number of recent events with complete follow-up.
- For a broader exploratory development sample, retain explicit development/later-period exposure tags. Any reuse of previously exposed issuers belongs in that disclosed development branch, not in the old quarantined validation branch. Never remove human protections or quietly relax quarantine to enlarge a purported fresh test.
- Keep fixed-time exits initially. Exiting when the activist leaves or a campaign succeeds requires a separate point-in-time exit definition and disclosure evidence; no hindsight-based campaign-end dates.
