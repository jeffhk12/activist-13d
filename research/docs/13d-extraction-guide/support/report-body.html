Understanding 13D filings—and extracting information we can actually use

Start with who owns what, why they acquired it, and what they specifically want to happen. Keep those answers separate from whether the investor is an activist hedge fund and whether the investment will work.

This guide explains the disclosure, proposes readable attributes, and illustrates the pitfalls using the ten pilot filings already discussed. It does not replace the current codebook or label the protected human-first review cases.

Recommended starting point

Our present intent categories are useful as subcategories, but they start too far into the decision. Before asking “board representation or capital return?”, we need to know whether the passage describes an outside investor requesting change, an existing executive receiving shares, a sponsor completing a transaction, or someone reserving the right to act later.

Build a factual ownership-and-event record first; add an evidence-based interpretation second. For the first review screen, show the following questions in ordinary language. Keep detailed technical fields behind the screen rather than make the reviewer understand a database.

Question on the review screenWhat the answer should contain
Which company and shares?Issuer, exact security class, identifier and any unresolved identity conflict.
Who is involved?Reporting people/entities, fund versus adviser roles, and their relationship to the issuer.
How large is the position?Reported shares, class percentage, ownership date and voting/disposition powers, separately for each person.
How did the position arise?Purchase, conversion, compensation, distribution, sponsor holding or another disclosed transaction.
What specifically does the investor want?A short action statement naming the actor and target, or an explicit insufficient-evidence state.
What stage is it at?Considering, proposing, requesting, agreeing or completing an action; preserve conditions and dates.
What supports that answer?Exact passage, source document, relevant exhibit and unresolved contradictions.
What still needs checking?Missing references, uncertain entities, security matching and external data needed for investability.

This is a proposed display and attribute design. It is not a finding that these filings predict returns.

1. What a 13D means

Schedule 13D is a beneficial-ownership disclosure. Generally, acquiring beneficial ownership of more than 5% of a covered equity class triggers reporting, subject to the rules and available Schedule 13G alternatives. The covered universe is not simply “all common stocks”: the rule specifies covered equity and excludes non-voting classes. The percentage concerns a particular class, not automatically the entire company's economic ownership. [1]

Filing a 13D does not prove activism. For example, an otherwise passive investor using the relevant 13G exemption can become subject to 13D upon reaching 20%. Individuals, corporate controllers, transaction participants, advisers and investment vehicles also appear in these disclosures. We should not define an activist by form type or a hedge fund by a familiar-sounding name. [1]

Beneficial ownership also differs from a simple brokerage-account balance. Voting or disposition power, indirect control and certain rights to acquire shares can matter. One block can be reported by a fund and by its adviser or controlling person. Those reports can overlap; adding the percentages may double count.

The document tells us what the reporting parties disclose at that time. It does not reveal all private intentions, certify that a proposal will happen, supply a complete investment history or forecast returns. The research opportunity is to organize the disclosed facts and distinguish economically different situations.

2. Where the information lives

The form provides a useful reading order. However, an item can incorporate another item or exhibit rather than repeat it. The full evidence packet is therefore the unit we read; the Item 4 paragraph alone is often insufficient. The table paraphrases the official form requirements, not a claim that every filing supplies each answer clearly. [2]

SectionUsually requested informationWhy it is useful / main trap
Main coverIssuer, class, CUSIP, contact, triggering-event date, amendment number and applicable prior-13G checkbox.Identifies the disclosure. The communications contact may be a lawyer, not the investor. Event date is not publication time.
Person cover pagesReporting-person name, group checkbox, funds and person-type codes, jurisdiction, share amounts, voting/disposition powers and percentage.One record per reporting person. Preserve overlap and exclusions; do not collapse everything into one “fund stake.”
Item 1: Security and IssuerSecurity-class title, issuer name and principal office.Supports identity. It does not validate a historical ticker, market capitalization or tradable share class.
Item 2: Identity and BackgroundBusiness, entity organization, office, individuals' occupation and citizenship, and specified proceedings during the preceding five years.Helps distinguish the vehicle, manager and controlling people. A mentioned person is not automatically a reporter; the proceedings disclosure is not a general quality score.
Item 3: Funds or ConsiderationAcquisition amount/source, relevant borrowing, and non-purchase consideration.Separates cash purchases from awards, conversions or transaction exchanges. Acquisition expenditure is not current market value.
Item 4: PurposeAcquisition purpose and plans/proposals concerning specified corporate or ownership changes.Main source for stated objectives. Generic possibilities, completed history and someone else's plan require different treatment.
Item 5: Interest in SecuritiesPerson-level holdings and powers, rights to acquire, recent trades, rights to proceeds, and applicable exit-below-threshold date.Supports stake and trading context. Transactions cover the past 60 days or since the latest 13D, whichever is shorter—not necessarily the complete position history.
Item 6: Contracts and RelationshipsVoting, transfer, financing, options, derivatives, pledges and related arrangements.Can explain control, restrictions and economic exposure. Derivative notional is not automatically reported beneficial ownership.
Item 7: ExhibitsRelevant agreements and incorporated documents, including joint-filing documents.The actual demand may be in a letter. A joint-filing agreement establishes a filing arrangement, not necessarily a campaign demand.
SignaturesCertification, signers, titles, authority and signature date.Keep signatory identity and signature date separate from investor identity and public availability.

Codes we can decode without an LLM

Funds codes include SC (subject company), BK (bank), AF (affiliate), WC (working capital), PF (personal funds) and OO (other). Multiple codes can apply. Read the narrative for the actual financing structure.

Person codes include IA (investment adviser), IV (investment company), HC (parent holding company/control person), CO (corporation), PN (partnership), IN (individual), BD (broker dealer), BK (bank), IC (insurance company), EP (employee benefit plan/endowment), SA (savings association), CP (church plan) and OO (other). There is no “activist hedge fund” code; IA does not mean hedge fund. Store the original code and its mechanical decoding separately from any business-model inference. [2]

Format changes matter

Structured filing became mandatory on December 18, 2024, following a voluntary period. Exhibits remain outside that structured-format requirement. XML improves access to fields but does not decide whether a sentence expresses activism. The SEC's technical inventory also records multiple-CUSIP support added in March 2026. A durable design should preserve format/version information and allow repeated identifiers; inspect the applicable schema before implementing version-specific fields. [3] [4] [5]

3. Recommended attribute dictionary

Use four distinct labels for where an answer comes from: filing fact (what is disclosed), normalized fact (a documented conversion or match), interpretation (a rule-based reading of meaning), and external enrichment (information obtained elsewhere). A cleanly formatted model answer is still an interpretation until checked.

“Core” means useful for the first review version. “Later” means retain the source now but add the structured detail when it serves a defined analysis. All fields need an evidence or provenance link, an as-of date where relevant, and a missingness state.

Attribute / priorityPlain-language definition and representationSource / safeguards
Filing identity · CoreAccession, form, initial/amendment status, amendment number and validated predecessor links.SEC metadata and cover. An accession identifies a filing, not an entire campaign.
Public availability · CoreSEC filing date and acceptance timestamp with timezone, separately.Recorded SEC metadata. Do not turn a calendar filing date into an assumed market-open execution time.
Other dates · CoreCover event date, transaction dates, letter dates and signature date as separate fields.Exact source and role for each date. A date without its meaning is unsafe for event studies.
Issuer identity · CoreBody name, header name, CIK and historical identity-match status.Retain conflicts, name changes and alternatives. Do not silently replace a body issuer with a header name.
Security identity · CoreExact class title; raw and normalized CUSIP list; currency if disclosed; identifier confidence as a verification state.Cover/Item 1. Preserve leading zeros. A valid-looking CUSIP is not a confirmed historical trading match.
Reporting parties · CoreOne entity record per reporter: legal name, CIK when available, original person-type code and jurisdiction.Cover/Item 2. Keep signers, contacts and other mentioned entities in separate roles.
Party relationships · CoreFund managed by adviser; GP controls vehicle; individual controls manager; direct holder versus indirect reporter.Item 2/5 and footnotes. Record relationship edges with evidence instead of guessing one campaign name.
Relationship to issuer · CoreDisclosed executive/director, sponsor, controller, outside holder, mixed group or unresolved; retain relationship timing.Interpretation supported by role passages. A director installed through an earlier campaign differs from a pre-existing executive; use contemporaneous evidence.
Reported ownership · CoreShares, percentage, class, person and as-of date; preserve reported group totals separately.Cover/Item 5. Never automatically add person rows. Separate reported amounts from calculated quantities.
Ownership powers · CoreSole/shared voting and sole/shared disposition amounts; acquisition rights, exclusions and disclaimers.Person covers/footnotes. These are related but not interchangeable measures of control.
Ownership basis · LaterFiler-stated shares outstanding, reference filing/date and treatment of exercisable rights.Item 5/footnotes. Reversing shares ÷ rounded percentage is only an estimate, not an authoritative denominator.
Acquisition mechanism · CorePurchase, conversion, award, distribution/spin-off, sponsor subscription, contribution/exchange, other or unresolved; multiple events allowed.Item 3 and transaction descriptions. Do not classify every increased holding as an open-market conviction purchase.
Funding and consideration · LaterSource codes, narrative source, amounts/currencies, borrowing and consideration type.Item 3. Investor acquisition borrowing is different from demanding the issuer change its capital structure.
Recent transactions · LaterPerson, trade date, buy/sell or other event, quantity, unit price, venue/method and notes.Item 5(c)/schedules. Preserve ranges/averages as reported. Do not derive complete cost basis from a partial reporting window.
Stated purpose · CoreA short faithful description of why the filing says the position is held.Item 4 plus incorporated material. Keep source wording and assistant summary side by side.
Specific action · CoreActor + action + issuer/target + stated condition, with one row per distinct action.Interpretation grounded in exact passages. “May discuss various matters” cannot become “seeks board seats.”
Action topic · CoreReadable group and detailed subtype from an explicitly versioned taxonomy.Multiple topics allowed. Topic alone cannot establish activism or outsider eligibility.
Action stage · CoreConsidering, proposed/requested, agreed, implementing, completed, withdrawn or unclear; separate historical versus current.Tense, attribution and date. Completed spin-off is not automatically a new demand.
Engagement channel · CoreDisclosed discussion, letter, shareholder proposal, nomination, solicitation, litigation, agreement or unclear.Do not infer a proxy contest from general voting language or infer an action merely from a joint filing.
Agreements and constraints · Core summary; Later detailBoard rights, voting arrangements, standstill, lock-up, registration rights, settlement/financing terms and relevant dates.Item 6/exhibits. Distinguish enforceable dated terms from aspirational targets and unresolved references.
Evidence completeness · CoreWhich documents were supplied/read, which references matter, what is missing or unreadable, and whether a contradiction remains.A successful parser does not certify full evidence. Negative intent labels require adequate review.
Extraction provenance · CoreQuote, document ID, character offsets, raw/canonical hashes, source URL, rule version, producer, timestamp and review status.Exact quote equality is mechanically testable. Semantic support still needs checking.
Market and filer enrichment · Later, separateHistorical security type, ticker, market cap, liquidity, sector, manager mandate and documented prior activism.Dated external sources. Do not describe them as fields extracted from 13D.

A flat spreadsheet can display a summary, but the underlying records should preserve repeated people, holdings, actions and exhibits. Otherwise a single row forces us to hide overlapping ownership or combine different actors' plans.

4. Make action categories understandable

The official Item 4 topics cover acquiring/disposal of securities; mergers/reorganization/liquidation; material asset transfers; board/management changes; capitalization/dividends; other material business/structure changes; charter/bylaw/control-related provisions; delisting; deregistration; and similar actions. These are disclosure topics, not ten activist labels. [2]

For a human-facing screen, I recommend grouping our existing research subcategories under the following simpler questions. This is a proposed display crosswalk, not an implemented v2 protocol.

Readable groupQuestion / examplesExisting v1 subcategory
Change the people in chargeSeek director seats, nominate directors, replace executives?board_representation; leadership_change
Return money or change financingRequest dividends/buybacks, recapitalization or other capital-structure changes?capital_return; capital_structure
Sell, combine or separate assetsSeek sale of the company, divestiture or another asset transaction?sale_of_company; asset_transaction
Change how the business runsRequest a concrete change in operations or business strategy?operational_change
Change shareholder rights or governanceRequest a voting, charter, bylaw or other governance change?governance_change
Another explicit objectiveA specific supported objective outside the above definitions?other_stated_objective

Keep investor buying/selling intentions as a separate ownership-action field. “May sell shares” is not an issuer capital-return request. Likewise, “used bank financing to buy shares” is not a demand that the issuer borrow money.

Require a complete action statement

Use the template: [named actor] [requests/proposes/agrees to] [specific action] concerning [named issuer/target], [condition or timing if stated]. Then attach the exact evidence. If one of those components is uncertain, preserve the uncertainty rather than fill the gap from reputation.

A specific conditional plan can be meaningful; boilerplate retaining the right to discuss many possible actions is different. Distinguish specificity from stage, and stage from success. Several action topics can coexist. Avoid a single “activism strength” or “positive sentiment” score until its definition and usefulness are independently demonstrated.

5. What the ten exposed pilots teach us

These are teaching examples drawn from the previously discussed pilot, not fresh labels for the human-first sample. The supporting quotations below are checked against the stored canonical document text. The comments are assistant interpretations and open review questions, not accepted human reference labels.

The pilot contains ten selected initial filings spanning HTML/text and XML formats. Selection was for inspection, not population estimation. Its parser coverage cannot tell us what fraction of all 13Ds are activist, how accurate the proposed categories are, or whether one type earns higher returns. A field that the parser did not recover may still exist in the source.

Three design lessons

Read the exhibit when the item points there. In the Bulldog example, a short Item 4 reference is not the substantive shareholder proposal. An extractor that stops there could miss useful information, while one that fills in a remembered campaign could invent it. Store both the reference and the actual proposal evidence.

Retain the ownership graph. In the Chavant/Mobix filing, sponsor shares also appear within an individual's reported beneficial ownership, alongside warrants. Separate person records, rights and denominator bases are necessary; summing cover percentages would misrepresent the disclosed position.

Preserve identity disagreements. The Chavant header/body naming difference and the PreCheck reference in the Gillen example are not cosmetic nuisances. They can affect issuer matching and relationship classification. Record the competing names and resolve them with evidence instead of choosing the most plausible name.

Open the existing full ten-filing review pack for longer source text. The quotation provenance records document IDs, exact character spans and hashes.

6. Group stocks and filers without mixing definitions

Your interest in activist hedge funds is a useful research direction. It requires several independent questions, rather than a single “high-quality event” label.

Grouping axisSuggested values / evidenceWhy keep it separate?
Issuer and instrument typeOperating company, REIT, closed-end fund, BDC, SPAC or unresolved; then exact common/ordinary or other security class.Corporate structure and security rights differ. Some classifications need external confirmation; never assume all 13Ds concern comparable common stocks.
Filer business/roleInvestment adviser, fund vehicle, corporate holder, individual, sponsor, other or unresolved.Legal form, reporting role and investment mandate are different attributes. A partnership can be many kinds of investor.
Investment mandateDocumented hedge fund, other investment manager or unresolved, with dated evidence.13D does not have a reliable hedge-fund checkbox. External firm materials or regulatory records may help but must be interpreted and dated.
Issuer relationshipPre-existing management/control, sponsor, outside shareholder, campaign-related role, mixed or unresolved.A board role can precede the event or result from an earlier campaign. Relationship timing matters.
Current disclosed actionSpecific issuer-directed objective, generic possibilities only, none stated after adequate review, or cannot determine.A recognized activist manager may file without a new specific demand. An unfamiliar holder may make a concrete demand.
InvestabilityHistorical market cap, dollar volume, spread, listing and verified security coverage.These govern feasible execution; they do not define whether the filing contains activism.

A proposed target group is documented investment-manager/fund involvement + a supported issuer-directed objective + a resolved issuer relationship + an eligible, historically matched security. Whether to exclude particular insider or campaign-related board situations is a research-policy decision to define before measuring returns. Keep unknowns visible and retain the broader baseline so we can measure what the filter removes.

Market cap: useful as a separate filter

A market-cap filter can help define the investable universe, but market cap is not normally a 13D extraction field. Obtain a dated historical value from a market-data source with clear coverage. If calculated, specify price date, issuer/class share-count basis, currency and corporate-action treatment. A reported beneficial-ownership percentage is not market capitalization, and the report's position value is not issuer value.

Initially display size and liquidity as review groups rather than select a return-maximizing cutoff. Later, predefine thresholds and examine sensitivity. Use information available at the event; a present-day company classification or surviving ticker can introduce hindsight and survivorship bias.

“High quality” should be replaced by measurable ingredients: clear stated action, documented actor, observable stage, adequate evidence and tradability. None by itself proves a profitable investment.

7. Rules that make extraction reproducible

  1. Freeze the source. Preserve raw submission, retrieval provenance and a canonical text representation for every document. Hash both raw and canonical content; stable character offsets refer to one exact text version.
  2. Read the whole relevant packet. Inventory references and exhibits. Mark missing material before assigning a negative intent label. Document boundaries and Item headings are extraction aids, not completeness guarantees.
  3. Separate raw and normalized fields. Keep the original name, CUSIP and date string beside any standardized value. Record every mapping rule and unresolved alternative.
  4. Attribute each claim. Identify the reporter, actual actor and target. A quoted issuer statement or description of another shareholder is not automatically the reporting group's objective.
  5. Keep time explicit. Distinguish past completed transactions, current requests and contingent future possibilities. Record when the statement became public, not just when the underlying event occurred.
  6. Preserve multiple answers. Multiple reporting people, share classes, actions and conflicting passages need repeated records, not a forced single choice.
  7. Use explicit missingness. No parser output is not a negative finding. Require evidence completeness before interpreting silence.
  8. Version definitions before reuse. Freeze codebook, examples, prompt and schema together. Record which version produced each extraction; do not overwrite v1 labels under a changed meaning.
StateWhat it means
Present and supportedThe fact/action is supported by an attributable passage.
Not stated after reviewAdequate relevant evidence was reviewed and no qualifying statement was found. This is not proof of private intentions.
Ambiguous / contradictoryAvailable evidence supports competing readings or identities.
Unavailable / unreadableA material referenced document or usable text is missing.
Not parsed / not reviewedThe process has not completed the relevant step. Do not substitute zero, false or passive.

Amendments and event timing

A 13D/A may update selected items rather than repeat the full original filing. Link it to earlier evidence only through validated relationships and only using information public by the observation time. A filing, an investor, an issuer/security and a campaign are different units. Several amendments from one situation are not independent campaigns.

The usual initial deadline changed from ten calendar days to five business days effective February 5, 2024; material amendment timing changed from “promptly” to two business days. Apply the appropriate historical regime when describing the data. A long cover-date-to-filing gap alone does not establish a violation: event meaning, reporting trigger and applicable rules must be checked. [3]

For price studies, define disclosure time and the first feasible execution session before testing. Acceptance after the close, weekends and exchange holidays matter. The cover event date can precede public disclosure and should not silently become t=0.

8. Review efficiently, including with an LLM judge

The best way to reduce manual work is to make each output narrow and checkable. A judge asked “is this a good activist opportunity?” combines factual support, taxonomy and investment forecasting. A judge asked “does this passage support this named actor requesting this action?” has a clearer task.

CheckSuitable methodWhat it cannot establish
Required fields, types, code values and exact quote matchingDeterministic validation.A genuine quotation can still be irrelevant or misinterpreted.
Actor, target, specificity, stage and category fitSeparate model judge under the same explicit rubric; allow supported / contradicted / insufficient evidence.Model agreement is not ground truth, particularly when both models share a mistaken assumption.
Material reference coverageDocument inventory plus targeted human review of unresolved references.A reference detector may miss a reference; an empty list is not proof of completeness.
Reliability measurementHuman references on a held-out, appropriately sampled set; field-level error reporting.Reviewing only disagreements does not measure errors where both models agree.

Give the judge the source bundle and proposed answer, not investment returns. Ask for the specific unsupported element and its counter-evidence, rather than an unrestricted essay. A different model can provide another view, but independence is not guaranteed. Record model identifiers, prompt/input hashes and settings; reproducibility means an auditable process, not a promise of identical outputs from a changing hosted model.

Preserve the current human-first cases. Develop explanations using the already exposed pilot, freeze human references before showing model answers for protected cases, and keep independent evaluation separate. A small calibration batch can reveal confusing definitions; it cannot establish precise overall accuracy. Audit a sample of agreed positives, agreed negatives and abstentions as well as disagreements.

The earlier LLM judge research note reviews relevant papers. ARES illustrates combining automated evaluation with human annotations; JudgeBench examines difficult judge decisions. These are methodological precedents, not validation of 13D extraction. [6] [7]

9. How the attributes support the investment study

Keep two research questions distinct: Can we extract the disclosure reliably? and Does that information improve decisions beyond a simple filing-event baseline? Better extraction need not produce higher returns; a profitable-looking filter can also be caused by selection or hindsight.

The baseline can use a predefined eligible filing universe, historical security matching and a fixed event/holding convention. The NLP extension adds only disclosure-time attributes such as actor role, acquisition mechanism, specific action and stage. Compare both on the same eligible universe, timing and cost assumptions. Report exclusions and missing-data coverage rather than let the NLP sample quietly become easier to trade.

Pre-filing and post-filing charts remain useful previews. They should not determine extraction labels. Keep future prices, later settlements and campaign outcomes outside the evidence seen by the extractor or human reference reviewer. Group related filings to avoid counting one situation repeatedly; reserve chronological out-of-sample investment evaluation after the extraction method is frozen.

Useful early research comparisons include specific versus generic objectives, external-manager involvement versus pre-existing insider context, and acquisition mechanisms. These are hypotheses to test, not conclusions from the ten pilots or the purposively selected manager-and-action candidates.

10. A practical next version

I recommend refining the review interface before expanding semantic classification. Keep the first screen focused on the eight questions at the beginning of this guide. Display the exact source next to every proposed answer, with a visible “needs evidence” state. Detailed ownership, agreement and provenance records can sit in linked tables.

  1. Agree on the core vocabulary. Define issuer relationship, acquisition mechanism, specific action and action stage with boundary examples. Treat the six readable action groups as a display layer over detailed definitions.
  2. Prepare a new version as a draft. Add the proposed fields and validation rules in a separately versioned schema/codebook. Preserve the existing v1 protocol and earlier outputs.
  3. Use the exposed ten as demonstrations. They can explain rules and expose design problems, but cannot serve as unexposed evaluation.
  4. Continue human-first development review. Use the protected pack for its intended reference workflow; resolve confusing definitions before launching automated assessment.
  5. Evaluate each field before scaling. Report factual extraction accuracy, semantic category errors, abstentions and missing-reference rates separately. Sampling must include excluded/noncandidate cases to estimate missed events.

Completed for this report: official-source research, the proposed field dictionary, a display crosswalk, and exact-quotation validation for the ten exposed examples. Not completed: a v2 classifier, new human labels, external stock/filer enrichment or a validated investment filter. No paid model calls or protected-case classifications were run.
