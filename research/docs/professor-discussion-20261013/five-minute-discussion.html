13D data idea: a five-minute conversation

Private Markets Modeling discussion, 13 October 2026. Evidence through 12 October.

Two-page data handout · One-page speaking guide · Machine Learning brief · Project home

Use three minutes to explain; leave two for feedback. The main question is whether the investor definition and observation construction are sensible, and where extracted information might add value. The older four-page valuation version remains available as an earlier draft.

0:00-0:35 | The idea

I am building a machine learning class project around 13D filings. The question is whether information about who is investing, their ownership and their stated plans can help select stocks after disclosure. I want to test a potential source of alpha, rather than assume that following an activist creates an edge.

0:35-1:15 | What I have done

I have a source-audited archive of about 75,600 filing records and have processed about 15,800 initial-filing candidates for factual extraction. Separately, the current return-data and feature checks leave 667 initial-event candidates. I have built ten structured inputs and prepared chronological training data. No predictive model has been trained, and the earlier simple-following work has not established a reliable edge.

1:15-1:50 | How I identify investors

I use 35 historical HFR snapshots to find candidate manager matches and distinguish activist, other Event-Driven and other strategies. I am concerned that a firm's strategy label does not necessarily describe a particular filing, and legal vehicles complicate identity. Most current observations remain other or unresolved; I am keeping them in scope.

1:50-2:25 | How filings become observations

My current row represents an eligible initial disclosure linked to a stock and a subsequent return window. The existing cuts include market-data coverage, prior investor-company history and overlapping positions. I want your view on which cuts are appropriate for learning a signal. Amendments may provide additional information, but they cannot be counted as independent new investments or used before they became public.

2:25-3:00 | Where I go next

First I will train a small neural network and simpler comparisons using rule-based facts. Then I will test whether validated LLM extraction of ownership relationships and stated intentions adds information. BlackRock publicly describes LLM-based investment text analysis, and specialist 13D research already exists. I would like to understand where you think a student project could make a useful contribution.

3:00-5:00 | Ask, then pause

Would you change the HFR investor definition or the way I construct observations, and where would you look for information the market may not already price in?

Follow the thread he picks. Optional prompts:

  • Investor definition: HFR categories, validated manager identities, or intent in each filing?
  • Observation design: retain a broader initial-event panel, with amendments as separately timed updates?
  • Industry practice: which extraction or signal questions would make this a useful, realistic project?

Do not read every count in the handout aloud. If interrupted, move directly to the main question.

Concepts to keep straight

An observation is one model row: here, an eligible initial disclosure with a stock, date and later return. A feature is an input known by that date. The target is the later return being predicted. The four investment decisions are downstream uses of predictions, not automatically four labels combined into one target.

Deterministic means the same code and input give the same result; it does not mean the result is true. An LLM can interpret relationships and language beyond fixed parsing rules, but may vary across runs. Repeated runs test stability; comparison with human source-based references tests accuracy. Those are different questions.

All records can inform the archive and history, while only covered, properly timed observations with outcomes enter supervised learning. Amendments can support a future update panel, without importing future information into initial rows. The inherited portfolio non-overlap rule is proposed for review, not already changed.
