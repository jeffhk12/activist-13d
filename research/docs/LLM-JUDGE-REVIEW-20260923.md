# Reducing manual review with an LLM judge

Literature reviewed: 2026-09-23. Status: research note and proposed design, not an implemented or validated evaluation protocol. No filing was labeled for this note; no model API was run. The human-first 52-candidate pack remains unexposed and paused.

An independently prompted second model can check an extractor's claims against filing evidence and route difficult cases to a person. It can reduce manual review, but agreement between two models is not evidence of correctness on its own. A human calibration set and a separate probability-based audit remain necessary for a defensible accuracy estimate. The studies below support investigating this workflow; none establishes its accuracy for Schedule 13D.

## Primary papers

| Paper | What the authors established | Relevance and limit for this project |
|---|---|---|
| Zheng et al., 2023, [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) | Strong judges aligned well with human preferences in the studied chat evaluations; the paper also documents position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases and reasoning limits. | Supports the feasibility of automated judgment. Chat preference agreement is not evidence of financial extraction accuracy. |
| Verga et al., 2024, [Replacing Judges with Juries: Evaluating LLM Generations with a Panel of Diverse Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796) | Across three evaluation settings and six datasets, a panel of smaller models from different families improved agreement and reduced intra-model bias relative to a single large judge. The paper's reported cost advantage is specific to its models and historical prices. | A different model family is worth testing. A panel is not statistically independent ground truth, and the paper leaves broader applicability and panel selection unresolved. |
| Saad-Falcon et al., NAACL 2024, [ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.20/) | Combines synthetic-data-trained lightweight judges for relevance and faithfulness with human annotations and prediction-powered inference. Its evaluations used a few hundred human annotations. | Closely related to checking whether source text supports an extracted claim. ARES is a trained RAG evaluation framework, not proof that a generic second-model prompt removes human labeling. |
| Angelopoulos et al., 2023, [Prediction-Powered Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633) | Uses a smaller trusted labeled sample to correct estimates derived from many predictions and construct uncertainty intervals. The main setup assumes independent samples from the same distribution and a prediction rule independent of the observed data; extensions address finite populations and certain distribution shifts. | Useful later for aggregate prevalence or quality estimates with a suitable audit design. It does not certify each filing's label, fix missing source evidence, or turn a convenience sample into a representative one. |
| Laskar et al., ACL 2025, [Improving Automatic Evaluation of LLMs in Biomedical Relation Extraction via LLMs-as-the-Judge](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1238/) | Evaluates eight judges, five generators and three extraction datasets. Judges usually performed below 50% accuracy in the studied unstructured setting; standardized output formatting improved performance. | Direct warning that extraction tasks can be much harder to judge than fluent conversation. Use structured field-level judgments and validate performance in this domain. Biomedical results do not quantify expected 13D accuracy. |
| Tan et al., ICLR 2025, [JudgeBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating LLM-based Judges](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=G0dksFayVq) | Tests objective correctness on difficult knowledge, reasoning, math and coding response pairs. Many strong judges struggled despite success on preference benchmarks. | Evaluate whether the judge catches substantive errors, not whether it prefers a convincing explanation. This benchmark is not a financial extraction benchmark. |

## Proposed workflow — project inference, not a paper result

1. **Keep deterministic checks first.** Validate schema, accession, document hashes, character offsets, and exact quotation equality. Keep dates and identifiers as strings with explicit missing/ambiguous states. A valid quotation proves that text exists, not that the proposed interpretation follows from it. These requirements already appear in the [project extraction workflow](../protocol/extraction-workflow-v1.md).
2. **Use a source-grounded second-model check.** Give a separately prompted judge the frozen codebook, full relevant evidence bundle and structured extraction. Hide the extractor's model/provider identity, human answers and subsequent returns. Ask separately whether evidence supports the label, the actor and target are correct, relevant contradictory language was missed, and required incorporated material is unavailable. Return supported / unsupported / cannot determine with exact cited spans and a brief evidence-based explanation. Treat document text as untrusted evidence, never instructions. Different providers may reduce some shared tendencies but do not establish error independence.
3. **Separate calibration from evaluation.** Human-label a development sample before exposing model answers for those examples; freeze human answers and revise the rubric using development disagreements. A first batch of 10–15 cases could reveal usability issues, but is only an illustrative pilot, not an adequate statistical validation claim. Freeze the protocol before evaluating a new, unexposed set. Related filings and repeated issuer/filer groups need leakage controls.
4. **Use two human-review queues.** Review disagreements, missing references and abstentions to resolve cases and improve development rules. Separately draw a probability sample with recorded inclusion probabilities from the intended population, covering agreed positives, agreed negatives and abstentions as well as disagreements. Weight stratified audit results appropriately. Disagreement-only review cannot detect shared mistakes or establish overall accuracy; changing the model after inspecting the audit requires a fresh evaluation.
5. **Report field-level performance and workload.** Measure precision, recall, evidence support, abstention coverage, unresolved reference rate and time spent by humans, with sample counts and uncertainty. Report weak or underpowered categories separately. Keep confidence supplied by a model distinct from empirical probability estimates. If the larger sample and statistical assumptions justify it, consider PPI for aggregate estimates; do not treat its output as per-filing certification.

## Application to the current review

The 52 manager-and-action candidates are a purposively selected development subset. An audit within those 52 describes that subset; it cannot establish recall over all 13D filings or detect all candidates rejected by the earlier screen. Estimating end-to-end recall requires a sampling frame that includes screen-negative filings. The larger 200-filing pack is also a development sample, not automatically a population-representative benchmark.

Preserve the current human-first pack. A future judge trial can follow frozen human labels on a development subset, while reserving separate unexposed cases for evaluation. This is a proposed reduction in the number of filings requiring detailed manual review, not permission to change the frozen protocol or expose the existing blind review to model answers.

Keep filer mandate, issuer relationship, stated objective and security type as separate fields. A filing can support a stated demand without establishing that its filer is an activist hedge fund, and it cannot establish that an investment is high quality or will earn positive returns. Missing external evidence stays unresolved. Judge validation and investment performance evaluation are separate studies.

No performance, labeling savings or preferred judge model has been measured in this project. The immediate useful experiment, once authorized and specified, is a small development comparison of deterministic checks alone versus deterministic checks plus one independent judge, followed by a separately designed audit before scaling.

ARES's paper describes roughly 150–300 human annotations in its limitations, with 300 used in key experiments. Its appendix reports weak discrimination between systems below approximately 100–150 in that setting. Those figures describe that study, not a sample-size rule for 13D. An initial 10–15-case development pilot tests feasibility only. [ARES, Sections 4.2, 7 and Appendix Table 3](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.20.pdf).
